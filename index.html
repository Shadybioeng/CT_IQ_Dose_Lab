Definitions used in this lab

  • Shared engine: scanner models, protocols, AEC behaviour and the dose and noise model are the same as in the CT Protocol Simulator, so both tools give the same CTDIvol, DLP and noise for the same settings.
  • AEC: Siemens CARE Dose4D keeps the quality reference mAs (effective mAs for a reference adult); Philips DoseRight scales mAs with the Dose Right Index (about 12% per step); GE SmartmA keeps the noise index and Canon SUREExposure keeps the target SD, both defined at the reconstructed slice thickness (Canon also includes the IR). With AEC on, mA follows patient size, rotation time and pitch.
  • Automatic kV (CARE kV, kV Assist, SURE kV): for each kV the mA that keeps the CNR of the standard protocol is found, with a limit on how much noise may rise (about ×2 for angiography, ×1.25 for contrast-enhanced organs, almost none without contrast). The kV with the lowest CTDIvol is chosen; the protocol kV is left only for a saving of at least 5%.
  • Noise (σ): standard deviation of HU at the image level. σ ∝ kernel factor × (1 − IR noise reduction) / √(detected photons × slice thickness). Effective mAs = mA × rotation time / pitch (Siemens term; Philips shows the same quantity as mAs/slice; GE and Canon show mA and mAs per rotation).
  • CNR: lesion contrast (HU, adjusted for kV: iodine and bone gain contrast at low kV) divided by σ.
  • SNR: mean HU of the background tissue of the task divided by σ (for example liver 60 HU / σ).
  • Spatial resolution: MTF50 and MTF10 of the whole system in line pairs per cm. They combine the kernel, the pixel size (DFOV / matrix) and the focal spot. z-resolution is the slice sensitivity profile (FWHM, mm), set by the acquisition slice width (detector rows), the reconstructed slice thickness and the pitch. The reconstructed slice can never be thinner than the acquisition slice.
  • Noise texture: peak frequency of the noise power spectrum (NPS). Iterative and deep-learning reconstruction move it to lower frequencies (blotchier noise).
  • Detectability index d′: a simplified non-prewhitening observer for the task lesion. It uses contrast after in-plane blur and z partial volume, σ, and the noise correlation length (from the NPS peak). AUC = Φ(d′/√2). d′ ≥ about 3 is usually reliable detection.
  • Dose: CTDIvol = nCTDIw(scanner, 16 or 32 cm phantom, 120 kV) × relative tube output at the chosen kV (0.32 at 80 kV, 0.62 at 100 kV, 1.44 at 140 kV) × effective mAs/100 × overbeaming factor. DLP = CTDIvol × (scan length + overranging) × number of phases. SSDE = CTDIvol × size factor (AAPM TG-204 body, TG-293 head). Effective dose E = DLP × k.
  • Temporal resolution: half-scan reconstruction needs about half a rotation, so temporal resolution ≈ rotation time / 2 for one X-ray source and rotation time / 4 for two sources. Multi-segment reconstruction (retrospective gating) can shorten it further, depending on the heart rate. ECG gating changes the dose: retrospective helical gating uses a very low pitch (about 0.2–0.3) and exposes the whole cardiac cycle; ECG-based tube current modulation lowers this by about 40%; prospective axial gating exposes only the chosen phase; high-pitch dual-source scanning gives the lowest dose. The gating dose factors are teaching approximations at equal image noise.
  • Images are mathematical phantoms, not patient images. Values are approximations for teaching; real scanners differ.
